** caribbean_10_model is now a genuine, stable steady state of the corrected
mortality code. Final biomass for the six FORCE-observed groups is 79-121%
of target (pred_eng 5.87/5.51, pred_grab 24.99/27.6, pred_inv 12.61/14.13,
pred_plank 0.64/0.55, parrotfish 34.38/30.56, herbs 1.47/1.5); growth (age at
maturity) matches closely for five of the six and reasonably (within ~3x) for
pred_grab after correcting its own age_mat target (see below). Verified via
repeated reefSteady() calls leaving biomass bit-for-bit unchanged. The
complexity-increases-biomass result central to the manuscript was explicitly
re-verified against this recalibrated model: Complex refuge gives ~3.2x the
total biomass and ~2.0x the total productivity of a Flat/non-complex reef,
with parrotfish dominant on Complex and grabbers dominant on Flat, matching
Chapter4.tex's described pattern -- this was the seventeenth session's
original alarm (a stale bundled model showed the pattern reversed), now
confirmed fixed. **

** Unlike caribbean_3_model, no calibration script survived for
caribbean_10_model -- only inst/archive/caribbean_10_model_untuned.rda. The
user provided the original thesis chapter (Chapter4.tex, Appendix4a.tex,
Appendix4b.tex, and PDF renders c4_parameters.pdf) including the original
tuning script (Appendix4b.tex/c4_parameters.pdf section 4), which followed
essentially the same recipe as caribbean_3's original thesis-era script
(newReefParams() -> calibrateReefBiomass()+matchBiomasses()+matchReefGrowth()
+reefSteady() iterated -> switch to competitive refuge -> tune erepro ->
reproduction_level=0.5 for all groups). This session's recalibration started
from the existing bundled model instead (per the established
caribbean_3-session precedent -- rebuilding from raw CSVs diverges
immediately under the corrected mortality), and needed real procedural
departures from both the original thesis recipe and caribbean_3's successful
recalibration recipe: **

1. Cross-checked inst/data-csv/caribbean_10_species.csv's biomass_observed
   against Chapter4.tex's own parameter table and the bundled model. The CSV
   had biomass_observed=10 (pred_crypt) and =40 (inverts) that appear
   nowhere in the thesis and didn't match the bundled model (NA for both,
   matching the thesis exactly). The user confirmed these were values they
   personally added to the CSV during earlier hand-tuning (to force better
   diets), not FORCE observations, and asked to trust the thesis instead --
   reverted to NA. eels/pred_crypt/farm_damsel/inverts are the four groups
   the thesis itself never assigned an observed biomass (visual survey
   methods excluded fish <10cm and struggled with cryptic groups).
2. satiation=TRUE for parrotfish/farm_damsel/herbs (same fix as caribbean_3's
   herbivores), but grounded differently: their stored h values already
   matched the thesis's own Table 5 exactly, so this activates
   thesis-derived parameters rather than introducing a new one.
3. matchReefGrowth()/matchBiomasses() had to be applied ONE SPECIES AT A TIME
   -- caribbean_3's batched-alternation recipe (which worked for its 3
   aggregated groups) destabilised this 10-group model within a single
   alternation even when restricted to just the 6 well-observed species.
   Growth-matching specifically only converged cleanly once tried AFTER
   biomass was already close to target (not from the raw bundled model) --
   order used: pred_eng, parrotfish, herbs, then pred_grab.
4. pred_grab (Lutjanus apodus) resisted every growth-matching attempt,
   including gentle partial corrections -- traced to a genuine, checkable
   error in the thesis's own stored parameters: its age_mat=2 target was
   inconsistent with its own stored von Bertalanffy growth curve (k_vb=0.1,
   l_max=60cm). Converting an independently-sourced FishBase maturity length
   (Lm=25cm) through that same growth curve gives an implied age at maturity
   of 5.4 years, matching almost exactly what the model kept naturally
   settling on (~6y) whenever growth was left unconstrained. Updating
   age_mat to 5.4 immediately resolved the instability.
5. eels and farm_damsel (no FORCE-observed target) crashed toward zero
   biomass under the satiation fix. Literature search (with two PDFs the
   user retrieved directly) found: Gilbert, Rasmussen & Kramer (2005,
   Environ Biol Fish 73:415-426) on moray density/biomass in Barbados
   (whole-guild target ~10 g/m^2, reserve-reef-appropriate); Vermeij et al.
   (2015, Mar Ecol Prog Ser 528:289-296) on Stegastes planifrons abundance
   in Bonaire specifically (same reef system as karpata_refuge), whose own
   data show damselfish biomass is suppressed by predator biomass above
   ~40 g/m^2 (this model's predator biomass: ~47 g/m^2) -- soft target 0.4
   g/m^2, the paper's own cited historical Bonaire baseline. Both were
   explicitly treated as soft/approximate, not exact targets: fully matching
   them cost ~8x accuracy on the six FORCE-observed groups, so a
   deliberately partial correction was used instead (eels~1.4, farm_damsel
   ~0.15 g/m^2 achieved, vs 10/0.4 literature targets) -- both citations
   added to vignettes/references.bib.
6. At the user's request, a second version matching the six FORCE-observed
   groups more tightly (ignoring eels/farm_damsel entirely) was produced
   with identical growth tuning, but NOT bundled as a second package
   dataset -- doubling the recalibration burden for every future formula fix
   was judged not worth it. Archived instead at
   inst/archive/caribbean_10_model_tight_biomass_fit.rds (load with
   mizer::readParams(), not data()/load()).

** IMPORTANT addendum: the manuscript figure (karpata_example.png) surfaced a
real, substantive finding, resolved with the user before this session ended --
read this before touching pred_grab or the figure again. **

Regenerating the manuscript figure for real (not just the diagnostic ratio
check) showed total productivity going from a clear Complex>Flat pattern to
essentially flat/neutral (ratio 1.005, vs biomass which stayed clearly
Complex>Flat at ~2x). This traced to ONE specific change: pred_grab's
age_mat correction (2 -> 5.4, see item 4 above) shifts its population
age-structure toward larger/slower-growing individuals under refuge
protection (P/B ratio drops from 1.46 on Flat to 0.69 on Complex for
pred_grab specifically), which increases its standing biomass but reduces
its total production -- confirmed causally by reverting just this one
parameter and rerunning the comparison (productivity ratio jumps back to
1.27 with the old, uncorrected age_mat=2). This is NOT a calibration bug;
it's a real consequence of a literature-grounded fix.

The user's read: their manuscript (a package paper, NOT the PhD thesis --
karpata_example.png is not from the thesis) already argues productivity can
decouple from biomass under some conditions, so this result may actually
support that argument rather than undermine it -- total productivity stays
similar but its species COMPOSITION shifts substantially (grabbers' share of
productivity drops from 40% on Flat to 27% on Complex, nocturnal
invertivores' share more than doubles from 9% to 20%), which is visible in
the productivity panel's internal banding even though the total bar heights
are similar. The user decided (2026-08-02) to keep the literature-corrected
version (age_mat=5.4) as the official caribbean_10_model/manuscript figure
for now, pending discussion with their supervisor. An alternate version with
pred_grab reverted to the uncorrected age_mat=2 was built and archived
alongside the real figure for that discussion:
  - inst/archive/caribbean_10_model_v2_pred_grab_age_mat2.rds (load with
    mizer::readParams())
  - vignettes/figures/karpata_example_v2_pred_grab_age_mat2.png
If the supervisor conversation changes the decision, rerun
karpata_example_fig.R-equivalent logic against whichever params object is
